# Verification: "Enoch's Perpetual 364-Day Sabbath & Festival Calendar" chart
### vs. the Book of Ḥanoḵ (1 Enoch) and the game's THE APPOINTED TIMES (`appointed-times.html`)

**Document examined:** a two-page chart titled *"ENOCH'S PERPETUAL 364 DAY SABBATH & FESTIVAL CALENDAR (Enoch's Year Ends On 3/16 — The Day That Has Equal Night & Day Hours & Minutes In Jerusalem)"* (ilide.info upload).
**Compared against:** the repo's own Enoch text (*The Complete Book of Enoch, SEV*, Jay Winter — the sun chapters, standard 1 Enoch 72–82), the Yoḇelim (Jubilees) 6 passages quoted in-game, the Qumran calendrical scrolls scholarship, and the reckoning implemented in `appointed-times.html`.

---

## 1. What the chart actually is

Decoded, the chart defines:

| Feature | The chart's position |
|---|---|
| Year length | 364 days = 52 weeks; 12 months in four quarters of 30 + 30 + 31 days (months 3, 6, 9, 12 have 31) |
| Anchor | Day 1 of month 1 is **always Gregorian March 17**; the 364th day is **always March 16**, called "the day that has equal night & day hours & minutes in Jerusalem" |
| Weekday of New Year | Month 1 day 1 is the **1st day of the week** (a "Sunday" start); Sabbaths run down a fixed 7th column |
| Pesach | 1/14 — which in this scheme **always falls on the weekly Sabbath** |
| Unleavened Bread | 1/15–21, with the **wave offering (firstfruits) on 1/15**, the first day of the festival |
| Omer count | Sabbaths marked (7), (14) … (49) from 1/15; **Feast of Weeks = month 3, day 4** (the 50th day) |
| Trumpets / Atonement / Booths | 7/1, 7/10, 7/15–21 + "8th Day Addition" on 7/22 |
| Leap handling | "Two days change names in the leap year to \*2/29 & \*3/7. The name \*3/14 (in the leap year only) & the name 3/15 (in every year) **do not exist**." |

The chart is internally consistent (from a 1st-day New Year, 1/14 is a Sabbath, 1/15 and 3/4 are both 1st days), but the leap rule is the tell: pegging a 364-name year onto a 365/366-day Gregorian year forces one or two real days each March to carry **no calendar name at all**.

---

## 2. Does it align with the Book of Ḥanoḵ?

### Where it genuinely aligns

Verified against the repo's Enoch PDF (chapter "The Luminaries" = standard 1 Enoch 72; Winter's SEV merges chapter numbers):

- **364 days, exactly.** *"…the year is exactly as to its days three hundred and sixty-four"* (1 Enoch 72:32); repeated at 74:10–13 (*"complete the years with perfect justice in 364 days"*) and 82:6. ✓ Both the chart and the game agree.
- **30-day months with four added quarter days → 31-day months at positions 3, 6, 9, 12.** The sun stays *"one-and-thirty mornings on account of its sign"* in the 6th, 4th, 1st and 3rd portals (72:13, 19, 25, 31), i.e. the last month of each quarter carries the extra day. ✓ Chart and game both place the 31-day months correctly.
- **Four seasons / four turnings.** The four added days *"sunder the four parts of the year"*, led by Milki'el, Helemmelek, Melejal and Narel (82:11–20). ✓ Both.
- **The year ends on the equal day.** 1 Enoch 72:32: at the end of the 12th month *"the night decreases and amounts to nine parts, and the day to nine parts, and the night is equal to the day"* — and only then is the 364-day year complete. The chart's central claim (year *ends* at equal day/night, new year begins the next day) **matches Enoch's text**. Day/night equality recurs at the end of month 6 (72:20), and the year opens with the sun in the **fourth portal** with days already lengthening (72:7–10) — i.e. day 1 falls just *after* the spring equality. ✓ The chart's anchoring logic is genuinely Enochic here.
- **"Equal day and night in Jerusalem" ≈ March 16–17.** This checks out astronomically: the *equilux* (equal measured daylight/darkness) at Jerusalem's latitude falls around **March 16–17**, several days before the March 20 equinox, because sunrise/sunset are measured from the sun's upper limb plus refraction ([timeanddate: Equilux](https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/equilux.html), [KosherJava: Equinox vs Equilux](https://kosherjava.com/2022/01/12/equinox-vs-equilux-zmanim-calculations/)). If one reads 72:32 as an *observable* sign, the chart's March 16 is a defensible reading.

### Where it does NOT come from Enoch

- **Enoch's Astronomical Book contains no festivals and no weekday scheme.** Nothing in 1 Enoch 72–82 names Pesach, the sheaf, Weeks, Trumpets, Atonement or Booths, and nothing fixes which day of the week the year begins on. Every festival placement and the Sunday-start on the chart is imported from elsewhere (Torah dates + a modern reckoning), not from Enoch.
- **The skipped day-names contradict the tradition's own core rule.** Enoch insists the four added days *are* counted — *"men go wrong therein"* precisely by leaving them unreckoned (75:1–2; 82:4–6) — and Yoḇelim 6:32 (quoted in-game) commands the year be *"three hundred and sixty-four days… they will not leave out any day nor disturb any feasts."* The chart's rule that Gregorian **3/15 "does not exist" every year** (and 3/14 in leap years) quietly inserts 1–2 real, lived days outside the count each March. Two consequences:
  1. The year as actually experienced is 365/366 days — exactly the "adding to the count" that Jubilees 6:32–38 warns against.
  2. **The chart's Sabbath column does not track a continuous seven-day week.** 365 = 52×7 + 1, so a Sabbath pegged to fixed Gregorian dates slips one weekday per year (two in leap years) against any unbroken week. A "perpetual Sabbath calendar" that breaks weekly continuity is the standard critique of equinox-pegged Enoch calendars ([Feldman, "The 364-Day 'Qumran' Calendar and the Biblical Seventh-Day Sabbath", *Henoch* 31 (2009)](http://www.ronhfeldman.com/uploads/2/2/1/9/22191114/364-day_calendar_and_sabbath_-_henoch.pdf)).
- **No intercalation is attested anywhere in Enoch, Jubilees, or the Qumran scrolls.** The 364-day year runs ~1.24 days short of the tropical year; how (or whether) it was ever realigned is an open scholarly problem — Ben-Dov doubts any intercalation existed, since admitting one would concede the divine calendar drifts ([Ben-Dov & Saulnier, "Qumran Calendars: A Survey of Scholarship 1980–2007"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1476993X08094026); [Wikipedia: Enoch calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enoch_calendar)). The chart's Gregorian pegging is one *modern* solution to that problem — it is not in the text.

**Verdict on Enoch alignment: the chart's skeleton (364/52/four quarters, 31-day months at 3-6-9-12, year ending on the equal day) is authentically Enochic. Its weekday anchor, festival dates, Gregorian pegging and skipped day-names are modern additions, and the skipped days actively contradict Enoch 75:1–2 / 82:4–6 and Yoḇelim 6:32.**

## 3. Does it align with the game's APPOINTED TIMES design?

`appointed-times.html` implements the **Qumran/Yoḇelim reconstruction**: year begins at the spring turning on the **4th day of the week** (the day the lights were set in the expanse, Berĕshith 1:14–19), quarters of 30+30+31, sheaf on **1/26**, Shaḇu'oth on **3/15**, and the promise that *"the sheaf is always waved on the first day of the week, and the fiftieth day always falls on the first day of the week."*

| Point | Chart | Game | Book of Ḥanoḵ | Qumran scrolls / scholarship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year length & structure | 364; 30/30/31 ×4 | same | same (72:32; 72:13,19,25,31) | same |
| Weekday of 1/1 | **1st day** | **4th day** | silent | **4th day** — the mishmarot cycles and Jubilees' creation-week logic ([Qumran calendrical texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qumran_calendrical_texts); [BAS: Deciphered scroll reveals 364-day calendar](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/news/qumran-community-364-day-calendar/)) |
| Pesach | 1/14 = **always the Sabbath** | 1/14 = 3rd day | silent | 1/14 = 3rd day; the whole point of the Qumran design is that **festivals never collide with the Sabbath** |
| Wave sheaf | **1/15** (first day of U.B.) | **1/26** | silent | **1/26**, the 1st day after the Sabbath that follows the festival week (Mishmarot D: barley firstfruits on the 26th — [Zadok priestly calendar summary](https://man-child.com/enoch-calendar/)) |
| Shaḇu'oth | **3/4** (50 from 1/15) | **3/15** (50 from 1/26) | silent | **III/15**, always a 1st day ([TheTorah.com: The Three Shavuot Festivals of Qumran](https://www.thetorah.com/article/the-three-shavuot-festivals-of-qumran-wheat-wine-and-oil)) |
| Trumpets / Atonement / Booths | 7/1, 7/10, 7/15–21 + 8th | same | silent | same |
| Solar anchor | year **ends** March 16 (Jerusalem equilux); day 1 = March 17 | day 1 ≈ **March 20** (equinox) | equal day/night is the *last* day of the year (72:32) | anchor practice unattested; the drift problem is unresolved in the sources |
| Extra-day handling | Gregorian day-names skipped ("3/15 does not exist") | silent annual re-anchor at March 20 | none — 364 only | none attested |

**Agreements with the game:** year skeleton, month lengths, the four turnings as days of remembrance, Trumpets, Atonement, Booths (+8th day), and the principle that a fixed calendar keeps every feast on a fixed weekday.

**Direct conflicts with the game — the three load-bearing ones:**
1. **Weekday of the New Year** (1st vs 4th day). Everything else cascades from this.
2. **Firstfruits/sheaf** (1/15 vs 1/26). The game's on-screen quotation of Vayiqra 23:11 ("on the morrow after the Shabbath") is read by the chart as "the morrow after Pesach-which-is-a-Sabbath," and by the game (with Qumran) as "the morrow after the weekly Sabbath following the festival."
3. **Shaḇu'oth** (3/4 vs 3/15) — a straight consequence of #2.

Also note: under the chart, Pesach falls **on the weekly Sabbath every single year** — the exact collision the 364-day calendar was prized at Qumran for avoiding. That is a strong internal reason to doubt the chart's weekday anchor as a reconstruction of the ancient system.

## 4. One thing the chart gets *more* Enochic than the game

The game anchors day 1 at **March 20** (`enochToday()` uses `new Date(year, 2, 20)`), i.e. the astronomical equinox. Enoch 72:32's sign is *equal day and night as measured* — which at Jerusalem is the **equilux, March 16–17**, not March 20. If the game wants its "TODAY" pointer to track Enoch's observable sign, day 1 ≈ **March 17** (the day after the equal day) is arguably the better anchor; the current anchor runs ~3 days late. This is the single defensible correction the chart offers.

A related implementation note (independent of which anchor is chosen): because `enochToday()` re-anchors every Gregorian year and wraps with `((d-1)%364)+1`, the 1–2 real days before each March 20 repeat day-numbers (two consecutive "day 1 / 4th day" displays at year end). Any solar-anchored 364-day display must choose one of three honest options — (a) a pure continuous 364-day cycle from a fixed epoch (weeks unbroken, but seasons drift ~1.24 days/yr — about a month per 25 years); (b) annual re-anchor (seasons fixed, weekly continuity breaks at the seam — current behaviour, and the chart's, hidden); or (c) intercalate **whole weeks** when the year-start strays too far from the turning (keeps both weekday alignment and seasons; proposed in modern models, e.g. [Pratt's Enoch calendar model](https://www.johnpratt.com/items/docs/2020/enoch_cal_model.html), but unattested in the ancient sources). If the seam days are kept, consider displaying them explicitly as "days of watching for the turning" rather than silently repeating day 1.

---

## 5. Bottom line for the developer

- **The chart is a modern, Gregorian-pegged "Enoch calendar" of the Sunday-start school.** Its 364-day skeleton and its year-ends-at-equal-day anchor genuinely match 1 Enoch 72. Its Sabbath column, festival dates and skipped day-names are not in Enoch, contradict Yoḇelim 6:32's "leave out no day," and break the continuous seven-day week.
- **The game's current design should stand.** Its reckoning (4th-day New Year, sheaf 1/26, Shaḇu'oth 3/15, feasts never on the Sabbath) is the one actually attested in the Dead Sea Scrolls' mishmarot and Temple Scroll and is the scholarly consensus reconstruction of the Ḥanoḵ/Yoḇelim calendar the game cites on-screen. Adopting the chart's dates would put the game in conflict with its own quoted sources (Yoḇelim 6:23, 6:30, 6:32).
- **Worth adopting from the chart:** nothing structural — but consider (1) moving the solar anchor from March 20 to ~March 17 (Jerusalem equilux, the day after Enoch's "night equal to the day"), and (2) handling the year-boundary seam days explicitly instead of the silent modulo wrap.
- **Do not adopt:** the 1st-day New Year, the 1/15 wave sheaf, the 3/4 Feast of Weeks, Pesach-on-the-Sabbath, or the "names that do not exist" leap rule.
